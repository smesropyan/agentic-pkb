# Voice: what to keep and what to reach for

`phrases.md` and `structures.md` say what to remove. They cannot say what good prose does, so text that passes every
check still lands somewhere between inoffensive and flat. This file is the other half.

## Rule 1: a voice sample beats every rule below

If the author gave a sample of their own writing, or if the target text sits inside a larger document they wrote, read
it before rewriting anything. Derive their patterns from their own sentences rather than from adjectives:

- How long is a typical sentence, and do clauses stack or stay separate?
- How does a claim meet its reason: same sentence, next sentence, or never?
- What punctuation carries the work: colons, commas, parentheses?
- Where does emphasis come from: word order, bold, or nothing?
- What figures do they allow, and how often?

Then preserve those patterns. A rewrite that removes every tic and also removes the author's habits has failed, because
the author will read it as somebody else's prose and put their own back.

## Do not strip

The rules are a checklist for a machine's tics, not for every construction that resembles one. Leave these alone.

**A gerund carrying a real action.** "Achieving a goal splits into two stages: finding how to solve the problem, and
implementing the solution." Nothing is nominalized away here; the gerunds *are* the two stages.

**A figure doing real work.** "The agent purges the wide context." "Creativity thrives on breadth." A figure earns its
place when the literal version is longer and says less. Cut the decorative ones and keep these.

**A long sentence whose clauses depend on each other.** Length is not a defect. Chopping a dependent chain into short
sentences loses the dependency and reads as a machine that learned one rhythm.

**A system as the actor**, where the system really acts. See the exception in `structures.md`.

**An absolute that is literally true.** "Nothing limits how many sessions an agent may hold." "The PKB executes
nothing." The lazy-extremes rule targets false authority ("everyone knows", "this always fails"), not a measured claim
about a bounded system.

**A term repeated on purpose.** Elegant variation is a defect in technical writing. If the author calls it a session
eleven times, that is the rule working.

## Patterns to reach for

These are additive. Removal alone will not produce them.

**Join the claim to its reason in one sentence.** "Keep a separate session for each goal, because a session holds its
own memory and manages its own context." The reason rides in the same sentence with `because` or `and`, rather than
arriving as a second clipped statement.

**Gloss a term in an appositive.** "a conversation with a Topic Expert, the agent that holds one subject." The
definition rides along instead of taking its own sentence.

**Use a colon to unpack.** "A session usually has an objective: a problem to solve or a question to answer."

**State an absence flatly.** "The PKB implements and executes nothing." Direct, and stronger than a hedge.

**Prefer the plain verb.** holds, supplies, carries, reads, writes. Not provides, comprises, leverages, facilitates.

**Name the concrete instance.** A named book, a named system, a real path. "In his book *Range*, David Epstein shows"
beats "research suggests".

## The failure this file exists to prevent

A pass that applies every rule in `phrases.md`, `structures.md` and `ste100.md` to prose that argues will produce text
that is cleaner and worse: the actor inverted, a condition flattened into a schedule, a heading whose meaning reverses,
and facts lost inside compression. When a mechanical fix would damage a sentence, keep the sentence and say why.
